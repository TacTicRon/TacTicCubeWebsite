How to Play

Learn TacTicCube in a few minutes. Spin the cube, slide in your pieces, and build your goal in a straight line!

1Meet the Cube

TacTicCube is tic-tac-toe inside a big 3D cube.

The big cube is made of little cubes called miniCubes. Each side of the big cube has its own color, so you always know which side you're looking at.

You play against the computer, called the A.I.

  • Your pieces are white squares.
  • The A.I.'s pieces are black circles.

↑ Back to steps

2Spin It

Drag your finger to turn the cube.

Look at every side before you move. A great move might be hiding around the corner!

Peek inside: The Slice button pulls the layers apart so you can see the middle of the cube. Hide Blanks hides the empty miniCubes so only the pieces show. Tap the button again to put the cube back.

↑ Back to steps

3Make a Move

Tap a miniCube on the outside of the big cube.

Your new piece slides into the big cube right where you tapped.

  • In Tic Tac Toe 3D, your piece goes in right away.
  • In the word and math games, a box of letters, numbers, or signs pops up. Pick the one you want to slide in. Changed your mind? Tap Cancel and tap a different miniCube.

↑ Back to steps

4The Push-Out Rule

Every new miniCube pushes another miniCube out the far side.

When your piece slides in, every miniCube in that same line moves over one spot. The one at the very end gets pushed out of the big cube.

5"Not Yet!" and "Not That One!"

Two moves aren't allowed. If you try one, the game tells you why.

Not That One!

You can't push out the piece the other player just put in. Give it a turn first.

Not Yet!

A player's piece can only be pushed out once that player has enough pieces on the cube. Empty miniCubes can always be pushed out.

  • 3×3×3 cube: at least 4 pieces
  • 4×4×4 cube: at least 5 pieces
  • 5×5×5 cube: at least 6 pieces

↑ Back to steps

6How to Win

Build your goal in a straight line, and you win!

Your goal shows on your player card. In Tic Tac Toe 3D it's three of your pieces in a row. In the word and math games it's the word or problem you chose.

A straight line can go:

  • across or up and down on a side,
  • straight through the cube from one side to the other,
  • slanted, corner to corner across a layer,
  • or all the way from one corner of the big cube, through the very middle, to the opposite corner.

Your goal can read from either end of the line. When you win, the winning line lights up.

Watch the A.I.! It's building its goal too. Sometimes the best move is one that blocks its line or pushes its piece out.

↑ Back to steps

7The Games

Tap New Match to pick a game, a cube size, and how tricky the A.I. should be. Every game uses the same moves. Only the goal changes.

Tic Tac Toe 3D

Get three of your pieces in a straight line.

■■■

TacTic WordFun

Type your own goal word, then spell it in a line. The A.I. gets a word too.

CAT

TacTic Sum-It-Up

Make up your own adding problem and solve it. Then build the problem in a line.

3+6

TacTic TakeAway

Same idea with taking away. Put the bigger number first, so the answer is never below zero.

8−5

TacTic MathemaTic

Use adding, taking away, times, or divide. Bigger cubes let you build bigger problems.

4×2

In the math games you solve your own problem first, just to check it. Then you build the problem itself in a line, not the answer.

↑ Back to steps

8Ending a Match

  • Win: build your goal in a line first.
  • Draw (a tie): each match has a move limit. Near the end a Hurry! countdown shows how many moves are left. If nobody wins in time, it's a draw. It's also a draw if one move finishes both players' goals at the same time.
  • Need a break? Tap Pause or My Matches. Your match waits right where you left it.
  • End Match: stops the match for good. After you've made a move, it counts as a loss.

↑ Back to steps

For Grown-Ups

The first match teaches itself. A new player's first match is always Tic Tac Toe 3D, with on-screen hints for spinning the cube and making a move. It's a great one to sit beside your child for.

Difficulty grows with your child. The A.I. has five growth levels: Seedling, Sprout, Sapling, TreeTop, and Forest. Start low and move up as wins come easier.

The math is real practice. In the math games, kids choose their own problem and must solve it correctly before the match starts. If they get it wrong, the game shows a counting or step-by-step hint.

Bigger cubes. The free version plays on the 3×3×3 cube. Pro adds the 4×4×4 and 5×5×5 cubes for longer words and bigger problems.
